## About Me

I'm an AI researcher at **KAIST COGI Lab**, exploring how principles of learning and memory in the brain can inspire more adaptive artificial neural networks.

My primary interest is **continual learning**: enabling models to acquire new knowledge without catastrophically forgetting what they previously learned. I investigate **synaptic plasticity and metaplasticity** as potential mechanisms for balancing stability and adaptability.

I hold an **M.S. in Artificial Intelligence** and a **B.S. in Computer Engineering**. My work spans research ideation, implementation, experimental evaluation, and research computing infrastructure.

## Research Interests

- **Continual Learning:** catastrophic forgetting, class-incremental and domain-incremental learning
- **Brain-inspired AI:** biologically motivated learning and memory mechanisms
- **Synaptic Metaplasticity:** balancing parameter stability and plasticity across tasks
- **Representation Learning:** analyzing how neural representations change during sequential learning

## Current Research

### Metaplasticity for Continual Learning

Investigating methods that selectively stabilize important model parameters while preserving the flexibility to learn new tasks. Topics include:

- **Selective weight stabilization:** identifying and preserving parameters with substantial task-related changes
- **Activity-dependent importance:** exploring neuron-activity-informed measures of parameter importance
- **Stability–plasticity trade-off:** assessing how different stabilization strategies affect learning and retention

### Experimental Evaluation

Evaluating continual learning methods through sequential vision-learning tasks, including **MNIST** and **CIFAR**, and exploring class/domain shifts. Comparisons include regularization-based and replay-based approaches.
